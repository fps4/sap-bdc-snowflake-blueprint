# Simulation — the three modes, actually executed

Generated by `make simulate`. The same consumer question — six periods of the journal, joined to material master, grouped and ranked — answered three ways against the same 1,200,000-row synthetic source. Then one row is changed in the source, and each mode is asked whether it noticed.

## What this proves, and what it does not

DuckDB plays both sides here: an attached read-only database is the SAP source, the working database is Snowflake, a Parquet file is the Delta-Sharing object. On one machine with one storage engine the **query times come out nearly identical by construction** — the real federation penalty is a network hop into a system that is also running the business, and no laptop reproduces that. Those milliseconds are printed below for completeness and should be ignored.

What survives the substitution is everything the modes actually differ in: what each one leaves at rest, what each one obliges you to operate, and which of them can see a change that was posted a second ago.

## What each mode makes you own

- **REPLICATE** — a second copy at rest, and a delta job that runs on every schedule tick. initial load 1,176,334 rows, then a delta upsert of 23,666 rows that repeats forever; the source change posted after the run is invisible until the next one.
- **FEDERATE** — nothing at rest and no schedule — but a share of the source system's load, paid again on every question. two views and no pipeline; the source change is visible immediately, because there is nothing in between to be out of date.
- **SHARE** — nothing at rest and no schedule — but the producer's refresh cadence, which is a dependency you do not control. 1,200,000 rows published once as 17.5 MB of columnar objects and read in place; the warehouse stores nothing, and the source change waits for the producer to republish — a share is not federation.

## The three sentences worth taking away

1. **Replication is the only mode that leaves a second copy behind.** 29.4 MB in the warehouse against 0.3 MB for federation and 0.3 MB for the share — and at the catalog's real sizes that ratio is measured in terabytes and a monthly bill.
2. **Replication is also the only mode with a recurring job.** The delta upsert took 0.04s here and runs on every schedule tick forever; the other two have no tick to run on, and therefore no 03:00 failure to triage.
3. **Only federation saw the change.** The share did not — it waits for the producer to republish — and the copy did not until its next delta run. A share is not federation with better economics; it is a copy someone else operates, and its freshness is their promise rather than your schedule.
